Dot product:

The dot product had a much better speedup compared to map

2. On the two problems, what was the relative speedup of
vectorized implementations on single-precision floating

Assembly:

Map polynomial: 27.7 / 12.2 = 2.27x

Vectorclass:
Dot product: 13.2 / 6.5 = 2.03x
Map polynomial: 23.7 / 11.5 = 2.06x

3. When timing your assembly (and vectorclass) implementations
and the implementations created by the compiler, you likely
saw that for the "dot product" problem, the C implementation
performed more like the non-vectorized assembly. For the
"map polynomial" problem, the C implementation performed
more like the vectorized assembly. Why was the compiler
able to vectorize one but not the other?

The dot product keeps adding to a running sum. The order
of the additions matters because changing it can give a
slightly different result due to floating point rounding.

With map polynomial, each element is calculated separately
and doesn't rely on the result of the previous iteration.
Because of this, the compiler can calculate multiple
elements at once without affecting the results.
